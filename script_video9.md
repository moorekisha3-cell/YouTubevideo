## SCENE 2 — WHAT IS A ROTH IRA AND WHY IT MATTERS (0:50–2:00)

> "Let's start with the basics. A Roth IRA is an Individual Retirement Account that you fund with after-tax dollars. You've already paid taxes on the money before it goes in. But here's the deal: from that point forward, every dollar grows tax-free. And when you retire and start taking money out — after age 59 and a half — you pay zero taxes. Nothing."

> "Compare that to a Traditional IRA or a 401k, where you get a tax break now but pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement. With a Roth, you flip it. Pay the taxes now — while your balance is small — and let decades of tax-free growth do the heavy lifting."

> "The Roth IRA is especially powerful for younger investors and anyone who expects to be in a higher tax bracket in the future. If your money is going to grow significantly over time — and it will — you want that growth to be tax-free."

## SCENE 3 — WHO QUALIFIES FOR A ROTH IRA? (2:00–3:00)

> "Before you open one, you need to know if you qualify. There are income limits."

> "For 2024 and 2025, if you're a single filer, you can contribute the full amount if your modified adjusted gross income — your MAGI — is under $146,000. The contribution phases out between $146,000 and $161,000. Above that, you cannot contribute directly to a Roth IRA."

> "If you're married filing jointly, the full contribution is allowed up to $230,000 MAGI. The phase-out runs from $230,000 to $240,000."

> "The annual contribution limit is $7,000 — or $8,000 if you're age 50 or older."

> "And one important rule: you must have earned income to contribute. Wages, salary, self-employment income — these all count. Investment income alone does not."

> "If your income is above the limit, don't worry — there's a strategy called a Backdoor Roth IRA that still allows high earners to contribute. We'll cover that in a future video."

## SCENE 5 — HOW TO OPEN YOUR ROTH IRA STEP BY STEP (4:00–5:15)

> "Here is exactly what you'll do when you open your Roth IRA."

> "Step one: Go to the brokerage website and select 'Open an Account.' Choose 'Roth IRA' as your account type."

> "Step two: Enter your personal information. You'll need your full legal name, date of birth, address, Social Security number, and employment information. This is required by law for identity verification."

> "Step three: Designate your beneficiaries. This is who receives the account if you pass away. Most people name a spouse or children. Don't skip this step."

> "Step four: Review and agree to the account terms and submit your application. You'll receive an email confirmation. Approval typically takes one to three business days."

> "Step five: Link your bank account. Once approved, go into the account settings and connect your checking or savings account using your routing and account numbers. This is how you'll fund the account."

> "That's it. Your Roth IRA is open and ready."

## SCENE 6 — HOW TO FUND YOUR ROTH IRA (5:15–6:15)

> "Now let's fund it. Here are your options."

> "ACH bank transfer — the most common method. Once your bank is linked, initiate a transfer from inside your Roth IRA account. Select the amount, confirm, and done. Funds typically arrive in 2–5 business days. Many brokerages allow you to start investing immediately even before the transfer clears."

> "Remember the annual limit: $7,000 for 2025 if you're under 50. You can contribute the full amount in one lump sum, or spread it out monthly — $583 a month hits the limit perfectly."

> "One of the smartest things you can do is set up automatic monthly contributions. Log into your brokerage, find the automatic investment or recurring transfer setting, and set it to pull a fixed amount from your bank every month on the same date. Automating this removes the decision — the money moves before you have a chance to spend it."

> "You have until Tax Day — typically April 15 — to make contributions that count for the prior tax year. So in early 2025, you can still contribute for 2024 if you haven't hit the limit yet."

> "Opening and funding your Roth IRA is step one. Step two is actually investing the money. Many people make the mistake of leaving cash sitting in their Roth IRA doing nothing — that money is not growing until it's invested."

> "For most people starting out, the simplest and most effective approach is a low-cost broad-market index fund or ETF. A few great options:"

> "VTI — Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF. Covers the entire U.S. stock market, thousands of companies, very low expense ratio. One fund, fully diversified."

> "FXAIX — Fidelity 500 Index Fund. Tracks the S&P 500, zero expense ratio at Fidelity. Incredibly efficient."

> "SCHD — Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF. If you're focused on dividend income in your Roth IRA — which is an excellent strategy since dividends grow tax-free — SCHD is one of the best."

> "If you want even more simplicity, a Target Date Fund automatically adjusts your investment mix as you approach retirement. Pick the fund with the year closest to when you expect to retire — Fidelity Freedom 2050, for example — and it handles the rest automatically."

> "The key is: don't let the money sit as cash. Get it invested and let time and compound growth do the work."
